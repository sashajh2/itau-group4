## Abstract

Deepfake detectors built on frozen pretrained encoders are usually evaluated by asking a global
question: are real and synthetic media separated in the embedding space as a whole? We show that
for three widely used encoders — HuBERT (speech), OpenL3 (audio), and SENet (visual) — this
global question gives a misleading answer. Measured globally across 238 videos and 16,046 segment
embeddings, real and fake samples are almost entirely entangled: silhouette scores against ground
truth are ≈0.04 for HuBERT and the cosine distance between real and fake centroids is 0.005 for
OpenL3, i.e. indistinguishable from zero. Measured *locally* — within a single content group
(all augmented variants of one source video) at a single 0.15 s timestamp — the same embeddings
separate real from fake with silhouette 0.64 (HuBERT) and 0.59 (OpenL3), positive in 100% and
99.9% of the 701 evaluated timestamps respectively. Authenticity is therefore encoded in these
representations as a *relative, content-conditional* displacement, not as an absolute region of
the space. We give three consequences of this observation, each supported by an experiment in
this work: (i) global summary statistics of embedding geometry are poor predictors of downstream
detector performance and can be actively misleading; (ii) objectives that attempt to convert the
local signal into a global one by learning a low-variance "real manifold" collapse, and the
standard diagnostics for that collapse (silhouette, clustering scores) report *improvement* while
it happens; and (iii) evaluation protocols that do not control the composition of the fake pool
or group data splits by source video produce accuracy figures inflated by 20–35 points. We argue
that audiovisual deepfake detection on frozen embeddings should be formulated as a
reference-conditional or set-based problem rather than as a global classification problem, and we
outline the experiments that would test this.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Synthetic audiovisual media has moved from GAN-based face swapping to end-to-end diffusion video
generation in a few years `[CITE: Goodfellow et al., Generative Adversarial Networks, NeurIPS 2014]`,
`[CITE: Ho, Jain, Abbeel, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models, NeurIPS 2020]`. Detectors
trained on the artifacts of one generator degrade sharply on the next
`[CITE: Wang et al., CNN-Generated Images Are Surprisingly Easy to Spot... For Now, CVPR 2020]`,
`[CITE: Corvi et al., On the Detection of Synthetic Images Generated by Diffusion Models, ICASSP 2023]`.
The response that has generalized best so far is to stop training the feature extractor: freeze a
large pretrained encoder and fit only a light classifier on top
`[CITE: Ojha, Li, Lee, Towards Universal Fake Image Detectors That Generalize Across Generative Models, CVPR 2023]`,
`[CITE: Cozzolino et al., Raising the Bar of AI-Generated Image Detection with CLIP, CVPRW 2024]`.

This raises a question that the field has largely answered by assertion rather than measurement:
**in what form does a frozen encoder actually carry authenticity information?** The implicit
assumption behind almost every method built on frozen features — linear probes, Mahalanobis
scores, one-class objectives, k-NN detectors — is that authenticity corresponds to a *region*
of the embedding space, so that a fixed decision surface can separate real from fake across
arbitrary content. This paper measures that assumption directly and finds it false for the three
encoders we use.

### 1.2 The local/global distinction

We distinguish two ways of interrogating the same embedding set.

- **Global analysis** pools all segment embeddings across all videos, subjects, scenes, and
  datasets, and asks whether the real and fake populations are geometrically distinguishable —
  via PCA structure, centroid distance, clustering agreement (AMI/ARI/silhouette), distributional
  divergence (KL/JS/Wasserstein), and pooled linear separability.
- **Local analysis** fixes a *content group* — in AVDeepfake-1M++, all ~10 augmented variants of
  one source video — and a single timestamp, and asks the same question within that set of ~20
  embeddings that share subject, scene, phonetic content, and recording conditions.

The local view holds content and identity approximately constant by construction. The global view
does not. If authenticity were an absolute property of the representation, the two views would
agree up to noise. They do not: they disagree by more than an order of magnitude.

This framing has a precedent that we think is under-recognized. Person-specific and
reference-based forgery detectors — models that compare a query video against reference footage
of the same identity — have consistently outperformed identity-agnostic detectors under
distribution shift `[CITE: Agarwal et al., Protecting World Leaders Against Deep Fakes, CVPRW 2019]`,
`[CITE: Cozzolino et al., ID-Reveal: Identity-aware DeepFake Video Detection, ICCV 2021]`.
Our measurements offer a representation-level explanation for why: those methods are exploiting
the local structure that the global view averages away.

### 1.3 Contributions

1. **A local/global analysis protocol** for frozen audiovisual embeddings, implemented over an
   HDF5 corpus of 0.15 s segment embeddings with content-group and per-timestamp annotations
   (§4, [embeddings/analyzer.py](../embeddings/analyzer.py), [embeddings/metrics.py](../embeddings/metrics.py)).
2. **The central empirical result**: authenticity is locally separable and globally entangled in
   HuBERT, OpenL3, and SENet, with the size of the gap differing sharply by encoder and modality
   (§5.1–5.3).
3. **A resolution of an apparent paradox** in our earlier results — OpenL3 shows essentially zero
   real/fake cosine separation yet supports a linear probe at 0.98 AUC. The signal is present but
   lives in a content-conditional direction, not in the direction of the mean difference (§5.3).
4. **Three negative results, reported in full**, each of which follows from the local/global gap:
   a variance-minimization "real manifold" objective collapses and its diagnostics lie (§5.6);
   unstratified fake pools produce ~95% accuracy that drops to 63–75% under stratification (§5.5);
   and segment-level cross-validation without grouping by source video reports AUC 0.989 that we
   believe is leakage (§5.5).
5. **A set of design implications** — reference-conditional scoring, set/MIL aggregation, and
   grouped evaluation — with the specific experiments that would test them (§6, §8).

### 2.1 Pretrained representations as detector backbones

The encoders we analyze: HuBERT, a masked-prediction self-supervised speech model
`[CITE: Hsu et al., HuBERT: Self-Supervised Speech Representation Learning by Masked Prediction of Hidden Units, IEEE/ACM TASLP 2021]`;

and SENet, a channel-attention CNN used here as a frame-level visual encoder
`[CITE: Hu, Shen, Sun, Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks, CVPR 2018]`. For comparison in the
embedding-selection experiments we also evaluate wav2vec 2.0
`[CITE: Baevski et al., wav2vec 2.0: A Framework for Self-Supervised Learning of Speech Representations, NeurIPS 2020]`
and MFCC baselines.

The "freeze the encoder, train a probe" recipe for detection is established in
`[CITE: Ojha, Li, Lee, CVPR 2023]` and `[CITE: Cozzolino et al., CVPRW 2024]`.

### 2.2 Reference-based and identity-conditional detection

`[CITE: Agarwal et al., CVPRW 2019]` builds per-person behavioral models;
`[CITE: Cozzolino et al., ID-Reveal, ICCV 2021]` learns identity-aware temporal embeddings and
scores a query against reference real videos of the same subject;
`[CITE: Haliassos et al., Lips Don't Lie: A Generalisable and Robust Approach to Face Forgery Detection, CVPR 2021]`
and `[CITE: Haliassos et al., RealForensics: Leveraging Real Talking Faces via Self-Supervision for Robust Forgery Detection, CVPR 2022]`
exploit structure learned from real footage rather than fake artifacts.
`[CITE: Feng, Chen, Owens, Self-Supervised Video Forensics by Audio-Visual Anomaly Detection, CVPR 2023]`
is the closest prior work to the "train only on real" objective evaluated in §5.6, and should be
discussed explicitly as a method that *succeeds* where our §5.6 objective fails — the difference
in formulation is the interesting part.

### 2.3 One-class objectives and representation collapse

Our authenticity head (§5.6) is a variance-minimization objective over real samples only, which
is structurally Deep SVDD `[CITE: Ruff et al., Deep One-Class Classification, ICML 2018]`.
That paper's *hypersphere collapse* failure mode is precisely what we observe, and its
prescribed remedies (no bias terms, no bounded activations) are a natural next experiment.
The broader collapse literature is directly relevant: dimensional collapse in contrastive
learning `[CITE: Jing et al., Understanding Dimensional Collapse in Contrastive Self-Supervised Learning, ICLR 2022]`,
and the decorrelation-based objectives we drew the orthogonality constraint from —
Barlow Twins `[CITE: Zbontar et al., Barlow Twins: Self-Supervised Learning via Redundancy Reduction, ICML 2021]`
and VICReg `[CITE: Bardes, Ponce, LeCun, VICReg: Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization for Self-Supervised Learning, ICLR 2022]`.
The identity head uses prototypical contrastive learning
`[CITE: Snell, Swersky, Zemel, Prototypical Networks for Few-Shot Learning, NeurIPS 2017]`,
`[CITE: Li et al., Prototypical Contrastive Learning of Unsupervised Representations, ICLR 2021]`.

### 2.4 Probing, and what probes do and do not show

The gap between "a probe can recover it" and "the geometry encodes it" is a known hazard in
representation analysis. Linear probes `[CITE: Alain & Bengio, Understanding Intermediate Layers Using Linear Classifier Probes, ICLR Workshop 2017]`
can succeed on information that no unsupervised structure reflects; control tasks
`[CITE: Hewitt & Liang, Designing and Interpreting Probes with Control Tasks, EMNLP 2019]`
were introduced to calibrate exactly this. Our §5.3 result is an instance of the phenomenon in a
detection setting, and the control-task methodology is the right way to make it rigorous
**[OPEN]**. Cluster-agreement metrics used throughout: AMI
`[CITE: Vinh, Epps, Bailey, Information Theoretic Measures for Clusterings Comparison, JMLR 2010]`,
ARI `[CITE: Hubert & Arabie, Comparing Partitions, Journal of Classification 1985]`,
silhouette `[CITE: Rousseeuw, Silhouettes: A Graphical Aid to the Interpretation and Validation of Cluster Analysis, J. Comput. Appl. Math. 1987]`.

### 2.5 Shortcut learning and dataset bias

The §5.5 results are a textbook instance of shortcut learning
`[CITE: Geirhos et al., Shortcut Learning in Deep Neural Networks, Nature Machine Intelligence 2020]`
and of dataset bias `[CITE: Torralba & Efros, Unbiased Look at Dataset Bias, CVPR 2011]`.
Mitigations worth citing as the principled version of our stratification:
`[CITE: Arjovsky et al., Invariant Risk Minimization, arXiv 2019]` and
`[CITE: Sagawa et al., Distributionally Robust Neural Networks for Group Shifts, ICLR 2020]`.

### 2.6 Set-based and multiple-instance aggregation

The §6 design implication points at permutation-invariant aggregation:
DeepSets `[CITE: Zaheer et al., Deep Sets, NeurIPS 2017]`,
attention-based MIL `[CITE: Ilse, Tomczak, Welling, Attention-Based Deep Multiple Instance Learning, ICML 2018]`,
Set Transformer `[CITE: Lee et al., Set Transformer, ICML 2019]`,
against the sequence baseline `[CITE: Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need, NeurIPS 2017]`.

AVDeepfake-1M++ `[CITE: Cai et al., AV-Deepfake1M: A Large-Scale LLM-Driven Audio-Visual Deepfake Dataset, ACM MM 2024]`
⚠ verify — the "++" version is a later release and needs its own citation; the base and ++
generator mixes differ (base: WAV2LIP/TalkLip visual, SV2TTS/VITS/YourTTS audio; ++ adds
Diff2Lip, LatentSync, F5TTS, XTTSv2). ShareVeo3 `[CITE: ⚠ verify — dataset source for Veo 3 video collection]`
is generated by Google Veo 3 `[CITE: ⚠ verify — Veo 3 technical report]`; Sora2
`[CITE: ⚠ verify — OpenAI Sora 2 system card]`.

Two structural properties of AVDeepfake-1M++ are what make the local analysis possible at all:

- **Multiple augmentations per source.** Each source video has ~10 perturbed variants (Gaussian
  blur, salt-and-pepper noise, compression, colour quantization, motion blur, …). All variants of
  one source share subject, scene, and semantic content and differ only in low-level signal
  properties *and* authenticity. This defines the **content group**.
- **Partial fake injection with annotated boundaries.** Fakes are made by injecting synthesized
  audio or video into real source material, with temporal boundaries recorded, so a given
  timestamp within a content group can contain both real and fake variants.

Only 238 of the 241 AVDeepfake-1M++ source videos survive the local-analysis filter (a content
group must contain ≥2 augmentations and a timestamp must carry both labels), yielding **701
timestamps** eligible for local analysis. This is the sample size behind every local statistic in
§5.2. **[OPEN]** 701 timestamps drawn from 238 videos are not independent; per-video clustering
of the statistics is needed before any of these means get error bars.

### 3.2 Encoders and segmentation

All video and audio is segmented at **0.15 s** and embedded with three frozen encoders:
HuBERT (768-d, audio), OpenL3 (512-d, audio), SENet (2048-d, visual). Embeddings are written to
HDF5 (`exports/deepfake_embeddings.h5`) with per-video groups carrying `num_segments`,
`num_augmentations`, `augmentation_info/types`, `augmentation_info/source_idx`, and per-segment
`labels/audio` and `labels/video`; a mirrored serverless PostgreSQL store supports retrieval
during training.

⚠ **Justify the 0.15 s choice in the final text.** The current draft inherits it as a given. If
there is a resolution/compute trade-off study behind it, it belongs here; if not, say so.

### 3.3 Notation

For source video $v$, augmentation $a$, timestamp $t$, let $z_{v,a,t} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ be the
segment embedding and $y_{v,a,t} \in \{0,1\}$ its authenticity label (0 = real). The **content
group** at timestamp $t$ is $G_{v,t} = \{ z_{v,a,t} : a = 1 \dots A_v \}$. The **source
embedding** $s_{v,t} = z_{v,a^\star,t}$ is the unperturbed, unmanipulated variant.

## 4. Analysis Protocol

### 4.1 Global metrics

Computed over the pooled embedding set (all videos, all augmentations, all timestamps).

- **Global PCA.** PCA fit on a class-balanced sample of up to 50,000 vectors; first two
  components visualized with points coloured by label
  ([`fit_global_pca`, `plot_global_pca`](../embeddings/analyzer.py)).
- **Clustering agreement.** k-means with $k=2$ on the pooled embeddings, compared against ground
  truth by AMI and ARI; silhouette computed both against ground-truth labels (`sil_gt`, the
  quantity of interest) and against the k-means assignment (`sil_clusters`, a convergence check).
  Cosine distance throughout ([training/disentangled/metrics.py](../training/disentangled/metrics.py)).
- **Distributional divergence.** KL, Jensen–Shannon, and Wasserstein distances between the real
  and fake distributions of distance-to-real-centroid, histogram-binned at 50 bins.
- **Separation metrics.** Mean distance of real and of fake samples to the global real centroid;
  their difference (**separation gap**, positive iff fakes are farther); variability ratio.
- **Pooled linear separability.** Cross-validated logistic regression (stratified $k$-fold,
  $k \le 5$) over the pooled per-timestamp augmentation sets
  ([`compute_aggregate_statistics`](../embeddings/analyzer.py)).

### 4.2 Local metrics

Computed independently for each eligible $(v,t)$ pair and then summarized across the 701 pairs
([`analyze_single_timestamp`](../embeddings/metrics.py)).

- **Similarity to source.** Mean cosine similarity of real variants to $s_{v,t}$ and of fake
  variants to $s_{v,t}$. Their difference is the **local gap** — the primary local statistic.
- **Intra-group cohesion.** Mean pairwise cosine similarity within the real variants and within
  the fake variants.
- **Inter-group separation.** Mean cosine similarity across the real/fake boundary.
- **Local silhouette.** Silhouette of the ~20 embeddings in $G_{v,t}$ against their true labels.
- **Local centroid cosine distance.** $1 - \cos(\bar{z}^{\text{real}}_{v,t}, \bar{z}^{\text{fake}}_{v,t})$.
- **Spread and effect size.** Per-class spread (norm of the per-dimension std) and a multivariate
  Cohen's $d$.

### 4.3 A necessary caveat on the local metrics

⚠ **This paragraph must survive into the final paper, prominently.** In AVDeepfake-1M++, a fake
variant differs from the source in two ways at once: it has been perturbed *and* the audio or
video content in the manipulated window has been replaced by synthesized content that says
something different. A content-sensitive encoder — and HuBERT is trained precisely to be
phonetically sensitive — will register a large displacement for the second reason regardless of
whether any synthesis artifact is present. **The local gap we measure is therefore an upper bound
on the artifact signal, and for HuBERT it is plausibly dominated by content change.**
**[OPEN]** The control that settles this is a *real-to-real content-mismatch* condition: measure
the local gap between the source and a genuinely real segment from a different point in the same
video. If that gap is comparable to the real-vs-fake gap, the local separability we report is
largely a content-change detector, which is a legitimate temporal-inconsistency signal but a very
different claim from "authenticity is locally encoded." **This control has not been run and it is
the single most important missing experiment in this draft.**

Sources: `results/*` — see Appendix A. ⚠ The full-corpus and 20%-subset columns disagree
substantially (AMI 0.111 vs 0.001). They were computed by different scripts on different sample

Three things are true across every column. Clustering agreement is at or near chance. The
silhouette against ground truth is at or below zero, meaning a typical sample sits closer to the
opposite class's cluster than to its own. And the **separation gap is negative** — fakes are, on
average, marginally *closer* to the real centroid than reals are. Any method premised on "real
samples occupy a compact region and fakes fall outside it" is starting from a geometry that does
not have that property.

**Table 2 — Global centroid separation and pooled linear separability, by encoder** (238 videos,
701 timestamps, 4,655 real / 6,046 fake augmentation-level samples).

| Encoder | Dim | Mean cos(real, source) | Mean cos(fake, source) | Global centroid cosine distance | Pooled linear separability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HuBERT | 768 | 0.9185 ± 0.155 | 0.6394 ± 0.145 | 0.296 | **0.822** |
| OpenL3 | 512 | 0.9976 ± 0.010 | 0.9922 ± 0.012 | **0.005** | **0.821** |
| SENet | 2048 | 0.9305 ± 0.092 | 0.9021 ± 0.098 | 0.034 | 0.675 |

The OpenL3 row is the paradox this paper resolves: **a real/fake centroid cosine distance of
0.005 — five parts in a thousand, entirely within the noise of the intra-class spread — coexists
with 82.1% pooled linear separability, matching HuBERT's 82.2% despite HuBERT having a 60×
larger centroid separation.** In a separate segment-level audio evaluation
(`results/audio/audio_linear_probe_results.csv`, $n=941$), the same encoder reaches ROC-AUC
**0.976**, above HuBERT's 0.958; a further evaluation on the OpenL3 segment corpus
(`results/experiment_base_embeddings.json`) reports linear-probe test AUC **0.991** against a
Mahalanobis AUC of only **0.707** and a 5-way 5-shot accuracy of **0.708**.

That last triple is the whole story in miniature. Mahalanobis distance is a *global* density
model: it asks whether a point is unusual relative to the pooled real distribution, and it scores
0.707. A linear probe is free to find any direction, including one that is only meaningful
relative to local content, and it scores 0.991. The information is there. It is not where the
global geometry looks for it.

**Figure 1** (`results/baseline/hubert/figures/global_pca_avdeepfake_only_hubert.png`) shows the
qualitative version: 16,046 HuBERT embeddings in the first two principal components, with fakes
smeared throughout the real manifold rather than occupying any separable lobe. The visible
structure — the diagonal band, the low-left cluster — tracks content and recording condition, not
label.

Read against Table 1: **HuBERT's silhouette against ground truth goes from 0.037 globally to
0.637 locally — a factor of ~17 — on exactly the same embeddings.** OpenL3 goes from a centroid
separation of 0.005, which reads as "no signal," to a local silhouette of 0.595 that is positive
in 700 of 701 timestamps. Whatever OpenL3 is encoding about authenticity, it is perfectly legible
once you stop averaging across content.

Note also what *does not* change between the global and local views for OpenL3: the local centroid
cosine distance (0.0052) is identical to the global one (0.005). The mean-difference direction is
uninformative at both scales. What makes the local view work is not a larger displacement but a
*consistent* one — the same small displacement, in a content-specific direction, for every
variant in the group. Silhouette picks this up because it compares each point to its own group;
centroid distance cannot, because it averages content-specific directions that do not align
across groups and therefore cancel.

**Figure 2** (`results/baseline/hubert/figures/exp1_hubert_gqpErbFnbiY_00015_seg13.png`) shows a
representative content group at $t = 1.95$ s: real variants at mean cosine similarity 0.987 to the
source, fakes at 0.148, with the real variants sitting essentially on top of the source point in
PCA space and the fakes dispersed.

**SENet is the informative exception.** Its local gap (0.030) is only 10× smaller than HuBERT's
but its consistency collapses: positive in 72.6% of timestamps against HuBERT's 96.0%, local
silhouette 0.155 against 0.637. Two readings are available and we cannot currently distinguish
them **[OPEN]**: either the visual manipulation (lip-sync region only) occupies too small a
fraction of the frame for a global-pooled visual encoder to register, or SENet — an ImageNet
classification backbone, not a forensic or face-specific encoder — discards the relevant
high-frequency detail. A face-cropped or forensic visual encoder would separate these
hypotheses.

⚠ Global silhouette was only computed for HuBERT (it comes from the disentanglement evaluation
pipeline, which was run on HuBERT). **Running the global clustering metrics for OpenL3 and SENet
is a cheap and necessary addition** — Table 4 is the paper's headline table and it currently has

The interpretation we propose: **these encoders represent authenticity as a displacement whose
direction is a function of content.** Formally, the hypothesis is that
$z^{\text{fake}}_{v,t} - z^{\text{real}}_{v,t} \approx \delta(c_{v,t})$, where $c_{v,t}$ is the
content and $\delta$ varies enough across content that $\mathbb{E}_v[\delta(c_{v,t})] \approx 0$.
Under that model every observation above follows: local silhouette is high, global centroid
distance vanishes, global clustering is at chance, and a linear probe still succeeds because it
can select a subspace in which the displacement is consistent even if the mean is not.

**[OPEN] This hypothesis is currently an interpretation, not a measurement.** It is directly
testable and the test is cheap: compute the per-content-group displacement vectors
$\delta_{v,t}$ and measure their pairwise cosine similarity across groups. Under our hypothesis
that distribution is centred near zero with a heavy tail; under the "global signal, low SNR"
alternative it is centred well above zero. **This experiment should be run before the paper is
written** — it converts the paper's central claim from inference to evidence.

4 layers, 8 heads, 30 epochs; [transformer_experiments/train_stratified.py](../transformer_experiments/train_stratified.py)).

| Encoder | Accuracy | FNR | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| HuBERT | 0.635 | **0.200** | 0.687 |
| OpenL3 | **0.755** | 0.460 | 0.688 |
| SENet | 0.665 | 0.340 | 0.663 |

OpenL3 — the encoder with essentially zero global separation — gives the *best* accuracy. HuBERT
— with 60× the global centroid separation — gives the best false-negative rate but the worst
accuracy. No single global statistic in Table 1 or 2 orders these correctly, and no encoder
dominates on both metrics.

Two further observations. First, all three models reach 99%+ training accuracy while test
plateaus at 0.63–0.76, on 600 training videos: these are overfitting regimes and the ranking
among them should not be over-interpreted **[OPEN]** — single seed, single split, no confidence
intervals. Repeat over seeds before drawing an encoder recommendation. Second, the error
*profiles* are complementary (HuBERT recall-oriented, OpenL3 precision-oriented), and the
probability-level correlation between HuBERT and OpenL3 probes is 0.824
(`results/audio/audio_correlation_matrix.csv`) — high but not saturating. A pairwise analysis
(`results/audio/pairwise_audio_analysis.csv`) shows the HuBERT ∨ OpenL3 union raises recall from
0.871 to 0.964 for a 4.8-point FPR cost, the only pairing in that table with a positive F1 gain
(+0.024). Late fusion is the cheapest available improvement and has not been run at video level.

### 5.5 Consequence II: without grouping and stratification, evaluation inflates

Three sampling regimes, same architecture, same budget:

**Table 6 — Effect of pool composition (video-level Transformer, 800 samples).**

| Regime | Result |
|---|---|
| **A.** AVDeepfake-1M++ only, unstratified | **Degenerate collapse** — HuBERT and OpenL3 predict all-fake |
| **B.** All datasets, unstratified | ~95% accuracy, FNR 0.07–0.09 across all three encoders |
| **C.** Stratified 50/50 fake pool | 0.635–0.755 accuracy (Table 5) |

Regime A collapses for a countable reason: AVDeepfake-1M++ contributes only 121 fake videos, so
the 200-sample test pool draws 30 fake videos with 3.3× repetition, and "predict all fake" scores
F1 = 0.667, beating any noisy partial solution the model can reach. Regime B's 95% is the
shortcut: **278 of 300 fake training samples came from ShareVeo3**, so the model learned a
ShareVeo3 signature, not a deepfake signature. The B→C drop of ~20–32 accuracy points is a direct
measurement of how much of the apparent performance was pool composition
`[CITE: Geirhos et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2020]`.

**A second, larger inflation comes from ungrouped splits.** A 4-fold MLP over concatenated
HuBERT+OpenL3+SENet features (3,328-d) at *segment* level reports accuracy 0.9958 ± 0.0003,
F1 0.855 ± 0.011, and **AUC 0.9887 ± 0.0005** (`results/concat_mlp_oversampling_4fold/metrics.json`).
That number should not be quoted as a generalization estimate. The split is
`StratifiedKFold.split(embeddings, labels)` over segments
([experiments/concat_mlp_experiment.py:479](../experiments/concat_mlp_experiment.py#L479)) — it
groups by neither video nor augmentation, so segments 0.15 s apart from the *same augmentation of
the same video* land in train and validation simultaneously. Given §5.2's finding that
within-content-group structure is the strongest signal in these embeddings, this is close to a
worst-case leak: the model is being tested on near-duplicates of its training points.
**Re-run with `GroupKFold` on source video id and report the corrected number**; the gap between
0.989 and the corrected value is itself a result worth publishing.

⚠ The same audit should be applied to the k-fold Transformer runs in `results/transformer/` and to
`results/seq_len_sweep/` before any of those numbers are cited. **[OPEN]**

### 5.6 Consequence III: converting the local signal into a global one fails

The natural response to §5.2–5.3 is to learn a projection that makes the local signal global: map
embeddings into a space where all real samples land in one compact region regardless of content.
This is the two-head disentanglement architecture of the parent thesis — a frozen encoder feeding
$f_{\text{auth}}$ and $f_{\text{id}}$, trained with prototypical contrastive loss on content
groups (identity), variance minimization over real samples only (authenticity), and a cosine
orthogonality penalty between the heads ([training/disentangled/losses.py](../training/disentangled/losses.py)).

It collapses. Table 7 gives the diagnosis.

**Table 7 — Variance-minimization objective, input vs learned $z^{\text{auth}}$ (HuBERT).**

| Metric | Input | $z^{\text{auth}}$ | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wasserstein (real vs fake) | 0.533 | 0.0015 | **−99.7%** |
| Mean distance to centroid, real | 5.70 | 0.32 | −94.4% |
| Mean distance to centroid, fake | 6.23 | 0.32 | −94.9% |
| Intra-group variance, real | 13.78 | 0.042 | −99.7% |
| Intra-group variance, fake | 19.98 | 0.058 | −99.7% |
| AMI | 0.111 | 0.099 | −11% |
| ARI | 0.066 | 0.022 | −67% |
| **Silhouette vs GT** | 0.037 | **0.059** | **+60%** |
| **Silhouette vs clusters** | 0.279 | **0.595** | **+113%** |

Everything collapses to a point — and **the two silhouette metrics go up**. This is the metric
hygiene lesson of the paper: silhouette is scale-invariant, so a space where every point has
collapsed onto every other point can report excellent cluster quality. Silhouette must never be
read without a companion scale statistic (Wasserstein distance, mean centroid distance, or raw
variance). The cross-dataset result is worse: on Sora2, real and fake mean distances become
0.32 and 0.32 — identical to three significant figures — and ARI goes negative
(`results/cross_dataset_metrics.json`).

**Root cause.** $L_{\text{var}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{real}}}\sum_{i: y_i=1}\|z^{\text{auth}}_i - \mu_{\text{real}}\|^2$
has **no gradient with respect to fake samples**. Their position is determined only by the
orthogonality term (which says nothing about authenticity) and by whatever bleeds through from
the identity head. Nothing pushes them away, and the trivial global minimum — map everything to a
constant — is reachable. This is hypersphere collapse in the sense of Deep SVDD
`[CITE: Ruff et al., ICML 2018]`; that paper's architectural constraints are the obvious untried
remedy.

**Attempted fixes and what they showed** (HuBERT, 20% subset: 59,792 real / 1,389 fake segments;
20 epochs; `results/fix1a_repulsion/`, `results/fix1_variants/`):

| Variant | Mechanism | Wasserstein | Mean cos(real,real) | Separation gap | Silhouette vs GT | Silhouette vs clusters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | none | 0.0017 | 0.99975 | 2.5e−5 | 0.048 | 0.146 |
| **Fix 1a** | hinge repulsion, margin 0.5, detached centroid | 0.0222 | 0.906 | 0.0086 | 0.038 | 0.173 |
| **Fix 1b** | unbounded distance maximization | 0.0120 | — | −0.0008 | −0.014 | **0.992** |

Fix 1a works as designed and is insufficient: it stops the collapse (real-to-real cosine
similarity drops from 0.99975 to 0.906, Wasserstein recovers 13×, separation gap turns positive)
but produces no real/fake separability — silhouette against ground truth does not improve. Fix 1b
is the more instructive failure: **silhouette against clusters reaches 0.992, and silhouette
against ground truth is −0.014.** The unbounded repulsion produced two beautifully separated
clusters that have nothing to do with the labels. Read `sil_clusters` alone and this is the best
run in the study.

Earlier runs (`ideas/disentanglement_fixes.md`) add a temporal dimension to the failure: the
identity head works well and stably (`sil_clusters` 0.67–0.70 across runs), while the authenticity
head peaks early and decays — the best run reached `sil_gt` = 0.127 at epoch 7 and fell to 0.090
by epoch 50, tracking the decay of the orthogonality loss from 0.028 to 0.0004. The cosine
orthogonality penalty vanishes once per-sample vectors are orthogonal, after which nothing
prevents identity information from bleeding back into $z^{\text{auth}}$. A cross-covariance
penalty in the style of Barlow Twins/VICReg
`[CITE: Zbontar et al., ICML 2021]`, `[CITE: Bardes et al., ICLR 2022]` stays non-zero under
per-sample orthogonality and is the recommended replacement. **[OPEN — not yet run.]**

**Interpretation.** We do not claim this objective cannot be made to work — `[CITE: Feng, Chen, Owens, CVPR 2023]`
demonstrates that real-only anomaly training *can* succeed for video forensics. We claim
something narrower and better supported: **an objective that defines authenticity as proximity to
a single global centroid is fighting the geometry documented in §5.1–5.3.** If the real/fake
displacement is content-conditional, there is no single global "real region" to compress toward,
and the only configuration satisfying the objective is the degenerate one.

### 5.7 Temporal order contributes little

If the signal is a per-segment, content-conditional displacement, sequence order should matter
less than architecture choice implies. The positional-encoding ablation
([transformer_experiments/compare_pos_encoding.py](../transformer_experiments/compare_pos_encoding.py);
AVDeepfake-1M++ only, 400 train / 200 test) is consistent with that, though it is confounded by
the Regime-A collapse of §5.5:

| Encoder | Positional encoding | Test accuracy | FNR | Test AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HuBERT | with | 0.500 (degenerate all-fake) | 0.000 | 0.538 |
| HuBERT | without | 0.575 | 0.110 | 0.543 |
| OpenL3 | with | 0.500 (degenerate) | 0.000 | 0.588 |
| OpenL3 | without | 0.500 (degenerate) | 0.000 | 0.506 |

Removing positional encoding does not hurt and, for HuBERT, is the only configuration that
escapes the degenerate all-fake solution (accuracy 0.575, FNR 0.110 vs a collapsed 0.500/0.000).
All AUCs are in the 0.51–0.59 band — near chance — so **this ablation is currently uninformative
about temporal order and should be re-run under the stratified Regime-C protocol before it is
reported as evidence** **[OPEN]**. Stated honestly, the result is: in a setting where the
positional model collapses, removing positional encoding does not make things worse.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 What the local/global gap implies for method design

If authenticity is a content-conditional displacement rather than an absolute region, three
design consequences follow.

**Score relative to a reference, not to a global model.** The natural detector is one that
compares a query segment against a reference for the same content or identity — which is what
`[CITE: Agarwal et al., CVPRW 2019]` and `[CITE: Cozzolino et al., ID-Reveal, ICCV 2021]` do, and
which our numbers give a representation-level justification for. The deployment obstacle is
obvious (a reference is not always available) and is exactly the gap a learned method should
target: learn to *synthesize* the content-conditional reference from the query itself, rather than
learning a global boundary. **[SPECULATIVE — this is a research direction, not a result.]**

**Aggregate over segments as a set, not a sequence.** §5.7's weak evidence plus the small training
sets (300–600 videos) point toward permutation-invariant aggregation: attention pooling, DeepSets
`[CITE: Zaheer et al., NeurIPS 2017]`, or attention-based MIL
`[CITE: Ilse et al., ICML 2018]`, which additionally yields per-segment anomaly scores useful for
the temporal-localization task that AVDeepfake-1M++ supports. The cheapest first check is
mean/std/max/min pooling into a small MLP: if that matches the Transformer, temporal modeling is
not contributing.

**Normalize within content group before pooling.** A mechanical version of the local view:
subtract the content-group mean, or the source embedding, before any global model sees the data.
This is a two-line change to the feature pipeline and directly tests whether the local signal can
be made globally usable without learning anything. **[SPECULATIVE — untested, but it is the first
experiment I would run.]**

### 6.2 Metric hygiene

Three concrete recommendations, each earned by a failure in this study:

1. **Never report silhouette without a scale statistic.** §5.6: a total collapse raised
   `sil_gt` by 60% and `sil_clusters` by 113%. Pair it with Wasserstein distance or mean centroid
   distance, both of which caught the collapse immediately.
2. **Never report cluster-based scores without ground-truth agreement.** §5.6, Fix 1b:
   `sil_clusters` = 0.992 with `sil_gt` = −0.014.
3. **Do not use the multivariate Cohen's $d$ as implemented.** Our
   [`cohens_d_multivariate`](../embeddings/metrics.py) divides a $D$-dimensional norm by a mean
   per-dimension standard deviation, so it grows like $\sqrt{D}$ and returns values of 24–2,611
   (HuBERT median 89). It orders encoders by dimensionality more than by effect size and should be
   replaced by a proper multivariate effect size (Mahalanobis $D$ with a regularized covariance)
   or dropped.

A fourth, from §3.3: `real_spread` and `fake_spread` reach exactly 0.0 at some timestamps, which
occurs when a class has a single member in that content group. Those timestamps should be
excluded from spread statistics rather than contributing zeros.

### 6.3 Relation to the parent thesis

This report is the analysis layer beneath Chapter 3 of `[CITE: Jovanovic-Hacon, Visual Deception
Detection for Financial Security: Homoglyph and Deepfake Identification, MEng thesis, MIT 2026]`.
That chapter proposes the two-stage disentangle-then-classify framework and its representation
quality metrics; §5.6 here is the empirical evaluation of its Stage 1 and reports that the
variance-minimization objective as specified does not achieve its goal. §5.1–5.3 offer a reason
why that is not merely a tuning problem.

## 7. Limitations

- **Scale.** 238 videos and 701 timestamps for the local analysis; 600-video training sets for the
  detection experiments. Single seed, single split, no confidence intervals anywhere. Every
  ranking in this draft is provisional.
- **Non-independence.** The 701 timestamps come from 238 videos and are not independent samples;
  all local means need per-video clustering before they carry error bars. **[OPEN]**
- **The content-change confound (§4.3).** The single most consequential open issue. Until the
  real-to-real content-mismatch control is run, we cannot separate "authenticity is locally
  encoded" from "content change is locally detectable."
- **Real-video monoculture.** Every real sample in the corpus comes from AVDeepfake-1M++. Nothing
  here tests whether the "real manifold" idea survives a second source of real footage, which is
  precisely the assumption §5.6's objective depends on.
- **Unresolved numerical discrepancy** between full-corpus and 20%-subset global metrics (§5.1).
- **Incomplete Table 4** — global clustering metrics exist only for HuBERT.
- **Leakage-affected results** (§5.5) remain in the results tree and must not be cited until
  re-run with grouped splits.
- **OOD evaluation is thin.** Sora2 appears only through the disentanglement pipeline. The local
  analysis cannot run on Sora2 or ShareVeo3 at all, because neither has the multi-augmentation
  content-group structure it requires — a structural limitation of the method worth stating
  plainly.

## 8. Conclusion and Next Steps

The three pretrained encoders we studied do carry authenticity information, and they carry it in a
form that global analysis is constitutionally unable to see: a content-conditional displacement
that is large and consistent within a content group and averages to nearly nothing across the
corpus. HuBERT's silhouette against ground truth is 0.037 globally and 0.637 locally. OpenL3's
real/fake centroid cosine distance is 0.005 at both scales, yet its local silhouette is 0.595 and
its linear probe reaches 0.98–0.99 AUC. The methodological consequences compound: global geometry
mispredicts downstream ranking, objectives that assume a global real region collapse, and the
standard diagnostics report success while they do so.

**Ranked next experiments.** Ordered by how much each would change the paper.

1. **The content-mismatch control (§4.3).** Real-vs-real across a temporal gap. Without it the
   headline claim is ambiguous. *Cheap — the data is already in the HDF5.*
2. **Displacement-direction consistency (§5.3).** Pairwise cosine similarity of per-group
   displacement vectors. Converts the paper's central hypothesis into a measurement. *Cheap.*
3. **Re-run the concatenated-MLP experiment with `GroupKFold` on video id (§5.5)** and report the
   corrected AUC alongside the leaked one.
4. **Complete Table 4** — global clustering metrics for OpenL3 and SENet — and resolve the
   full-corpus/subset discrepancy in Table 1.
5. **Content-group-normalized features (§6.1).** Subtract the group mean before a global probe.
   Directly tests whether the local signal transfers.
6. **Seeds and error bars** on Table 5 (stratified Transformer), then re-run the positional-encoding
   ablation (§5.7) under the stratified protocol.
7. **Set-based aggregation** (mean/std/max pooling → MLP, then ABMIL) against the Transformer
   baseline.
8. **Late fusion of HuBERT and OpenL3** at video level, motivated by the complementary error
   profiles and the 0.824 probability correlation.
9. **If the disentanglement line continues:** Deep SVDD architectural constraints, cross-covariance
   orthogonality with stop-gradient on $z^{\text{id}}$, and VICReg per-dimension variance terms —
   all specified in `ideas/disentanglement_fixes.md`, none yet run.
